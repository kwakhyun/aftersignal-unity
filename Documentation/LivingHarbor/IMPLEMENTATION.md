# Living Harbor

This update extends the preceding CyberConflict work in the same checkout.

- `CrowdFlow` spaces living pedestrians using a local spatial grid. Facility seating is assigned once per visitor, and failed street spawns are skipped instead of collecting at one fallback coordinate. Families preserve a walking formation when stopped.
- `PedestrianGround` and `PursuitPath` use supported steps, curb-top probes and obstacle detours. Rescue followers retain safe player breadcrumbs for recovery after a prolonged obstruction. The rescue objective supplies close-range first aid; boss completion no longer depends on unrelated wave counts.
- `CityActivityDirector` supplies couples and three-person families. Street commuters travel toward local work destinations in the morning and return toward home in the evening; this complements the existing residential routines rather than assigning every citizen an individual workplace interior. The local dialogue bank selects time, profession, age, relationship and incident-specific lines. These routines require no API calls.
- Football, baseball, basketball and racing retain the existing score/rules simulation. Ball presentation now distinguishes passing, dribbling, shooting, pitching and batted balls. Athlete poses add run cadence and sport-specific action offsets. Racing drivers evacuate during emergencies. Venue capacity is 180–360 visitors before safe-position filtering; occupancy is enabled near the player.
- `VenueSafety` pauses play and settlement on pitch intrusion or nearby injury. Players, staff and spectators have different evacuation dialogue and roles. Safety clearance resumes play with the existing score and bets.
- Incident map markers cover monster, gang, pirate and terrorist events. Contributing at least 100 monster damage or 25 hostile-human damage earns 650/90/160 credits upon neutralization (monster/gang/terrorist); credit is awarded once per opponent. Contributions older than 100 seconds expire. Main campaign targets retain their own quest rewards.
- Monster shockwaves and laser impacts can collapse nearby ordinary buildings. Buildings restore after 180 seconds, postponing while the player or a monster remains in the recovery area. Street props restore after their debris period. Combined meshes retain original topology and apply reversible cuts, including overlapping destruction. Cuts protect shared ground even when the building bounds include a below-ground foundation; unique venue facilities are excluded from collapse.
- Airports and ports offer immediate passenger boarding during dwell and waiting for the next departure. Cockpit/helm access points account for large craft geometry. NPC travellers walk to boarding points and have a terminal boarding recovery when scenery blocks the final approach.
- Coast guard cutters, navy destroyers and pirate interceptors use original Blender meshes with bridge, rails, deck equipment, turret and radar. Patrols seek opposing vessels; pirates can also attack civilian craft. Naval cannon shots use world collision and can be fired manually from the helm with the left mouse button. Vessel durability and sinking reuse the common transport systems.
- Army equipment remains at its existing installation. Aircraft move to the separate air base; coast guard and naval personnel occupy separate shore facilities. Full-map labels identify the bases.
- Harbor parcel repair moves obstructive high-rises into supported, unoccupied city parcels without extending the land. Standalone meshes move directly; combined building geometry is extracted above ground level while preserving the shared floor. The native world relocated 11 buildings and transferred 12 aircraft from the army installation to the air base.
- The fictional independent terrorist group uses a separate faction flag and sprite sheet. Random incidents occur at city streets and venue approaches, with destructible warning devices and firearm attacks. Police/SWAT dispatch follows the city reporting system. Army targeting explicitly excludes this group.
- Five original 4-direction/4-pose character sheets and the exact prompts are preserved alongside runtime imports. The source generator painted a checkerboard; the Unity importer converts the connected background to transparency.
- Closed Blender hull faces have their outward normals recalculated before FBX export. This prevents Unity back-face culling from removing decks that appeared intact in the Blender preview.

These are bounded local simulation systems: crowd avoidance is not a city-wide multi-floor NavMesh, and sports remain event-driven rules simulation rather than full ball-contact physics. The new naval and air facilities are compact operational compounds, not a new land expansion.

## Essential validation

`LivingHarborSmoke` is opt-in with `-living-harbor-smoke`; normal play does not run it. It suppresses player save writes and exercises crowd separation, a high curb, all six mission modes, reversible destruction, police-only terrorist targeting, rewards, all four spectator sports, passenger/cockpit/helm access, naval ballistics and new art. Native results and render captures are written under the supplied `-quality-output` path.

Release command: `AfterSignal.Editor.CyberSecurityImporter.BuildLivingHarborRelease` imports the new source art then builds Windows. Subsequent code builds can use `AfterSignal.Editor.ProjectBuilder.BuildRelease -incremental-player` to reuse unchanged build data; the normal default remains a clean player build.
